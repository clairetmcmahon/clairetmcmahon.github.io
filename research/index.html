Selecting Innovation Funding Mechanisms: A Framework for Policymakers

With Matthew S. Clancy, Matthew Esche, Siddhartha Haria, Christopher M. Snyder, and Caleb Watney

A framework to help policymakers choose how to fund innovation, from grants and contracts to prizes and advance market commitments.

Abstract

Innovation funding mechanisms differ in how they allocate risk, decision authority, and incentives. We develop a framework organized around three questions: Can a funder articulate the work to be done, specify a successful solution, and identify the most capable performers? The answers narrow the set of viable mechanisms and help guide the choice of how to fund research and development.

Review of Economic Design · 2024

Quantifying the Social Value of a Universal COVID-19 Vaccine and Incentivizing Its Development

With Rachel Glennerster, Thomas Kelly, and Christopher M. Snyder

Estimating the value of a vaccine that protects against existing and future variants, and the incentives needed to develop it.

Abstract

This paper uses Monte Carlo simulations to quantify the social value of a universal COVID-19 vaccine relative to variant-specific boosters. Even under conservative assumptions about the arrival of new variants, the estimated additional health benefits exceed $900 billion. The paper proposes an advance market commitment to incentivize development.

Disease Control Priorities 4 · Forthcoming

Investing in Vaccines to Mitigate Harm from COVID-19 and Future Pandemics

With Rachel Glennerster, Catherine Che, Sarrin M. Chethik, and Christopher M. Snyder

The social value of investing in vaccine research, development, and manufacturing capacity for pandemic preparedness.

Abstract

Rapid vaccination during pandemics can reduce mortality, economic losses, and societal disruption. Vaccine manufacturers, however, often lack sufficient incentives for speed and capacity expansion. This paper examines how governments and international organizations can strengthen these incentives and improve equitable vaccine distribution.

Working paper · 2024

IUDs to Degrees: The Impact of Access to Contraceptives on High School Graduation Rates

How access to long-acting reversible contraceptives affects educational outcomes, using evidence from Colorado.

Abstract

I study the 2009 Colorado Family Planning Initiative, which expanded the availability and affordability of long-acting reversible contraceptives. An event-study design uses geographic variation in access to participating clinics to estimate changes in high school graduation rates. The findings suggest that access to reliable, affordable contraception improves girls’ ability to invest in their education.

Working paper · 2024

One and Done? Evidence of Gender and Racial Tokenism on Corporate Boards

With E. Moon and Y. Li

Examining how board composition and outgoing directors’ characteristics relate to diversity in new appointments.

Abstract

Using panel data for S&P 1500 firms from 2000 to 2019, this paper studies diversity in corporate board appointments. A logistic model with two-way fixed effects and a bias correction evaluates how a board’s existing composition and outgoing directors’ demographic characteristics are associated with new appointments by gender, race, and ethnicity.
